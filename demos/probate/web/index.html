DE-221: spousal property

Ask to determine property passing to a surviving spouse or registered domestic partner, and/or confirm property they already own. This does not appoint an executor.

Open DE-221 helper

For a DE-221 spousal property petition

  1. Lodge original wills and codicils when required. California Probate Code § 8200 generally requires the custodian to deliver a will within 30 days after learning of the death. Copies attached to a petition do not replace lodging the original. Ask the clerk about submission and fees.
  2. Prepare the petition and supporting attachments. List the actual interest requested and the facts supporting it. Acquisition during marriage, deed wording and the legal character of property are distinct questions. A will-based request does not automatically settle the survivor's existing community interest.
  3. Prepare the proposed order. Use DE-226. Petition Attachment 7a maps to order Attachment 5a; petition Attachment 7b maps to order Attachment 7a. Copy the final descriptions and interests exactly. Leave the judge's signature and date blank.
  4. Prepare proof of a handwritten instrument if required. Use DE-135 with a complete copy as Attachment 4. The signer needs personal knowledge of the handwriting. Ask the court about proof for each will or codicil, and about DE-131 for a witnessed instrument.
  5. File the petition and obtain the case number and hearing date. Bring the proposed order and supporting proof if ready. A DE-120 notice alone does not start the case. Verify the current fee schedule and local documents. A fee waiver may be available.
  6. Serve the notice and petition. Complete DE-120 using the assigned hearing details. For DE-221, notice is generally at least 15 days before the hearing, to everyone entitled to it. Review petition items 9–11, heirs, beneficiaries, requests for special notice and any Attorney General requirement. Have a qualified mailer complete the proof after actual mailing, then file it.
  7. Check probate notes and attend as directed. Resolve deficiencies and submit the proposed order by the court's deadline. A completed helper does not mean the court has approved anything.
  8. After approval, obtain certified orders. Ask the recorder how to record the order for real property, and ask the DMV or institutions how to transfer or sell other assets. Separately check the assessor's death-of-owner reporting requirements. Mortgage payment and servicing obligations continue.
