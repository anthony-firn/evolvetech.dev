Security and verification limits

Security depends on the whole system: your device, recovery material, application code, signing keys, service providers and network. No EvolveTech app is represented as completely quantum secure or formally verified end to end.

Wallet and stablecoin

The built-in Fairground wallet uses Ed25519 spending signatures. A sufficiently capable future quantum attacker could break that scheme. Holding USDC stabilizes the asset’s target value; it does not change the wallet’s signer. Contract signatures use ML-DSA where supported, but authentic identity-key bindings still matter.

Algorand documents native Falcon-1024 account authorization. Changing an existing account’s authority requires compatible signing, network support and tested independent recovery. The present wallet has not been migrated to Falcon. No language change, signature wrapper or additional mining makes that migration happen automatically. Consensus, routing and issuer controls are separate dependencies.

Health records and HTTPS

Evolve Health uses AES-GCM encryption with classical Ed25519 and X25519 keys. Its sharing and identity system is not post-quantum secure. Encrypted records can still be exposed by device, key or recipient compromise. HTTPS protects connections under its negotiated cryptography; we have not established post-quantum TLS across every host, browser, relay and provider.

Browser storage and shared origins

The built-in Fairground account stores signing secrets in this browser. Apps hosted under the same website origin share a security boundary, even when their URL paths differ. A compromised script on that origin could access stored secrets. Rust or encrypted backups do not remove this risk. Keep substantial funds in a separate wallet. Moving sensitive apps to separate origins and requiring secure unlock would need tested account migration and recovery, and has not been implemented here.

Rust and proofs

Some projects already use Rust and WebAssembly. Rust can prevent classes of memory errors in safe code; it does not prove business rules, browser code, external cryptography or legal compliance. The fund-safety work separately models reserve preservation and spending restrictions with Bend proofs. A proof covers its stated model and assumptions. Testing Rust against a model is useful evidence, but is not a machine-checked proof that the two implementations are equivalent.

For the exact proof scope and reproduction commands, see the fund-policy sources. A deployed revision’s build metadata identifies its source; do not infer that every later source change is deployed.

Keep risk small

Use small amounts while the software matures, keep private backups, confirm payment details, and resume an uncertain conversion rather than creating a replacement. Heuristic content filters can miss harmful material; use the reporting channel to raise a problem.

Sources: Algorand post-quantum accounts, USDC terms, and Bend. Updated November 18, 2026.
