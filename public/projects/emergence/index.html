Emergence

Use a cheap model while its error is small enough. Unfold to a finer one where it fails. Bring that decision into fluid simulation, light, rendering and engineering design, with the uncertainty carried to the answer.

Design across every domain

Open the cross-domain workspace →   Drone example →   Shelter example →

One browser workspace designs anything that spans domains. A project is a file: the domains it uses, one design document per domain, the typed links between them, and what to watch. The domains are flight sizing, a power stage’s mechanical and electrothermal design, feature-tree parts, PCB, silicon, optics, fluids and architecture. Links convert units and refuse mismatched dimensions, and one solver iterates the loops between domains until they settle, then says so.

Two example projects show the range. An inspection quadcopter closes its weight spiral through eight domains: mass sets thrust, thrust sets the ESC current, the current heats the MOSFET and sizes its heat sink, and the heat sink is mass again. An edge-compute shelter runs its loop through a building: every node’s heat warms the shelter whose air is every node’s inlet, and the project’s own optimiser finds how many nodes fit. A blank project grows by adding domains, and each project carries its own guided tour of live commands.

A different project, the same system

Nothing in the workspace knows what is being designed. The shelter uses six domains and no flight or optics; its headline, sensitivity inputs, optimisation, tour and 3D layout all come from its project file. Projects live on your device with their own revision histories, download as files, and open in the Node CLI and MCP server unchanged.

Every link is visible

The System studio shows every link with its live value, every check across domains, a finite-difference sensitivity study through the full coupled solve, the optimisation the project defines, and the assumptions each answer inherits, which you can confirm one by one.

One kernel per domain

  • Parts are JSON feature trees (booleans, shells, arrays, gears, threads, sheet metal, pipe runs, lattices, wings, lenses) built and meshed watertight by the Rust CAD kernel in WebAssembly.
  • Boards route around obstacles on two layers. The kernel computes each trace’s resistance from its drawn geometry, field-solves microstrip impedance, and writes Gerber, Excellon and KiCad files. The package reads its own Gerber back against the drawing; it has not yet been checked by a CAM tool.
  • Chips synthesize Verilog onto transistor-characterised cells, checks the netlist against the RTL, and times it across nine supply and temperature corners at its own junction temperature. It then exports a GDSII floorplan that is read back to the nanometre, and images a layout clip with Abbe lithography and inverse mask optimisation.
  • Lenses trace real rays through catalogue glass. Air paths solve their operating point against a fan or a pressure source, bounding the flow wherever it sits between laminar and turbulent. Buildings solve each room’s heat balance.

Public reference designs

Each project file credits what its documents are built from. Both examples’ chips carry a public Tiny Tapeout dual-channel PWM motor controller (Apache-2.0), adapted to the native Verilog subset with every change listed. The drone’s camera is the Optiland gallery’s Cooke triplet (MIT), which traces to its published 50 mm focal length. The drone’s charging room and the shelter take the envelope of ASHRAE Standard 140’s BESTEST case 600.

The whole system in 3D

The render is assembled from each domain’s own geometry: kernel meshes of parts, heat sinks and lenses, plus boards, dies, batteries, rotor discs and rooms from their parameters. The project file says where each sits, and a site scope places the design at true scale in its building. Section, x-ray, picking and PNG capture are built in.

Everything is a command

Every edit in every studio is a typed, ranged command with one undo history across domains. The same registry runs from the in-page command line, browser scripts, a Node CLI and an MCP server, so an AI agent can design with the same tools and the same WebAssembly physics as a person. Each domain is a content-addressed document in every revision, so a whole project branches, merges by object, reviews and syncs between peers, and a history never joins another project’s. Exports carry a SHA-256 and are read back before they download.

Limits are stated in each studio. There is no KiCad import or STEP exchange yet; footprints are illustrative; optics are monochromatic and uncoated; lithography is scalar and in focus; the building is steady state; there is no PDK-backed place and route or sign-off.

Collaborative mechanical design

Cooling geometry, electrical losses, prescribed absorbed optical heat and external heat boundaries feed the Rust project graph. Native CAD produces STL, while dimensioned orthographic sheets use analytic design dimensions.

Shop drawings provide A3 vector SVG/PDF, first- or third-angle projection, material and finish, general tolerance, revision and preparer/checker fields. True-size DXF profiles and mechanical parts CSV support the handoff. Drafts and declared releases remain visible; geometry edits return released sheets to review. Optional donations reuse Fairground’s existing support flow and development fund.

A dimensioned handoff

Analytic geometry drives the sheet and profile. General tolerances, material condition, surface finish and the named checker are explicit fields; the first output is a draft for engineering review.

The same idea across systems

Many simulations spend their time resolving details that do not change the answer. The emergence engine asks each model for an error claim and a cost, picks the cheapest one meeting the request, and unfolds when its indicator or certificate fails. Expensive fine models can be folded into reusable closure tables checked on held-out points.

Claims say whether they are asymptotic, validated or rigorous. An audit compares selected answers with the reference model. If the requested accuracy is unreachable, the result says so.

Try it in the browser

  • Light and atmosphere: change a sphere's size, material or error tolerance. The real Rust ladder selects a Rayleigh expansion, a resonance-aware folded table, or the full Mie series. Every plotted result is checked against the series, with claimed and observed errors shown together.
  • Fluid motion: run the native f64 spectral CPU solver through WebAssembly on a small periodic grid. Compare a planar Taylor–Green vortex with its exact energy decay, or inspect a slice through the 3D vortex and its resolution warning.
  • Engineering design: edit fins and airflow in the original motor-driver cooling graph. Electrical losses, the temperature feedback loop and laminar channels feed requirements with uncertainty. A nominally passing trace drop is honestly undecided; out-of-regime flow makes the thermal result unavailable.

What the native workspace contains

The public repository brings together incompressible and compressible fluids, water/air interfaces, convection, photons and spectral rendering, heat and materials, chip and CPU design, CAD and a shared engineering project graph. The chip flow includes a small CPU running Fibonacci; the CAD examples link geometry to mass, printable parts and thermal requirements.

These systems have different maturity. Atmosphere and water optics are model building blocks, not a validated weather forecast. The repository documents which couplings exist and which remain incomplete.

Fluid motion you can inspect

A slice through an evolving periodic flow. The arrows show velocity in the slice; orange and cyan distinguish opposite vorticity. The exact planar preset provides a separate quantitative check. The 3D view uses the same CPU solver, with a small grid suitable for a browser.

Light transport and rendering

A retained native renderer output from the same workspace. Rust computes the scene geometry, light transport and final pixels. Wood and linen maps are generated appearance estimates; the scene is a rendering experiment with documented limitations. The source, settings and provenance are public.

Measured gains, with their limits

A retained native cloud-scattering benchmark was 10.7× faster than evaluating the full Mie series everywhere after a one-off table build; every sampled node agreed to 4.7 × 10⁻¹². Those are measurements for that benchmark and hardware, not browser speed claims.

For certified fluid resolution at Re = 200 and t = 0.5, a 10⁻³ requested L² tolerance selected 16³ with a 3.5 × 10⁻⁴ bound; the measured distance to a 48³ comparison was 8.7 × 10⁻⁵. Adaptive refinement did not beat the best fixed grid on Taylor–Green. Both the gain and that negative result are retained in the validation write-up.

What a claim covers

A numerical error claim applies inside a model and its assumptions. It does not certify a physical device or remove uncertainty from estimated material properties. The browser fluid grids are illustrative and manually selected; the native certified-resolution ladder is documented separately. The design graph carries the rigor of each contributing model through to its requirement status.
