Retirement Research

How much can savings pay each month, and how often does a plan break? A simulation engine that answers in spendable dollars after tax, with the assumptions and failures in view.

Open the income explorer →Source on GitHub ↗

Research, not advice.

Results are simulations on partly reconstructed market history, some strategies hold leveraged funds, and the tax model is a simplified US/California retiree. Use it to understand trade-offs, then check a plan with a fee-only fiduciary.

The problem

Most retirement calculators show a single return number. Retirees live on monthly cash after tax, and what hurts is a plan that breaks early in a bad decade. I wanted a model that measures those things directly.

What I built

  • A GPU simulator (PyTorch) that runs thousands of portfolios at once over reshuffled daily market histories from 1972 to 2026, including taxes by fund type, rebalancing bands, cash buckets and spending guardrails.
  • Searches that evolve fund weights and withdrawal rules, then a separate validation pass on 2,000 fresh 40-year histories and seven stress cases: weaker trend-following, a gold bear market, only recent history, and higher taxes.
  • A reproducible data pipeline that documents every reconstructed fund history, and keeps failed ideas in the record.
  • A local planning app (FastAPI, installable web app with iOS and Android shells), plus the browser explorer on this site.

Results worth noting

Against a 60/40 portfolio and an all-stock portfolio run with the same rules, the best tested strategies paid more in bad histories and broke less often in the base case. The stress cases show where that edge shrinks, which is the point of publishing them.

Engineering choices

  • Vectorised simulation on the GPU made full 40-year daily paths affordable across thousands of candidates.
  • Validation uses different random seeds from the search, so winners are not just tuned to their own test data.
  • Before publishing, the repository history was audited and rewritten to remove a private household's planning data.
