Health records · End-to-end encryption · Open source

Evolve Health

One place for a household’s health records: people and pets. Give the dentist, the eye doctor, the pharmacy or the kennel exactly what they need, let the people who care for you help, and record appointments so you don’t have to remember everything the doctor said. Records are encrypted in your browser; the servers that sync them only ever hold ciphertext.

Why I built it

Health records end up scattered across patient portals, paper folders and people’s memories. Every new dentist, specialist or kennel asks for the same history again, adult children helping an aging parent piece it together from pill bottles, and what the doctor said in a fifteen-minute visit is mostly forgotten by the parking lot.

I wanted one place that belongs to the person rather than to a portal company: free, open source, easy to run yourself, and built so that no one in the middle can read it, sell it, or hand it over.

Try it

Open Evolve Health → Choose Explore with sample data to load a fictional household that lives only in that tab. Jordan has their own records; their mother Rosa keeps her own vault and has made Jordan a manager; Biscuit the dog has vaccines, preventatives and a groomer. Jordan’s partner Alex has asked to see Biscuit’s records, so you can approve that request yourself.

To keep real records, create a vault: there is no email or phone number, just a passphrase and an Emergency Kit you print or save.

Pets too

A pet is a profile like any other, with species, breed, microchip and tag numbers, feeding and handling notes, vaccines with due dates, monthly preventatives and weights. Presets for a new vet, an emergency vet, boarding or daycare, a groomer, a pet sitter and a new owner send the right subset, so proof of vaccines for the kennel is two taps away. Exports use FHIR’s standard animal extension.

The right records for each professional

Choose who you are sharing with, such as a dentist, eye doctor, pharmacy, specialist, urgent care, physical therapist, school, vet, boarding kennel or groomer, and Evolve Health preselects what they usually need, with a sentence on why. A dentist gets allergies, blood thinners and bone medicines, heart conditions and joint replacements; a kennel gets proof of vaccines, feeding and the vet’s number. You review every item before anything is sent.

Records you mark private, such as mental health, reproductive and sexual health, substance use, genetic testing, and HIV and STIs, are never preselected for anyone. Records that clearly mention those topics are marked private automatically.

What the professional sees

The recipient opens a link and needs no account or app. The page decrypts the share in their browser, can print it, and downloads it as a FHIR R4 bundle that clinic systems can import. Send it as a revocable hosted link with an open count, a self-contained link or QR code, an encrypted file with a share code, or a printout. An optional 6-digit PIN, said out loud at the front desk, means a forwarded link alone is not enough.

Caregivers and family

Getting older, or caring for someone who is? Invite a son, daughter, spouse or home aide to a profile, each with their own vault. A Viewer sees everything; a Helper can also add records and record appointments; a Manager can also share with professionals and invite helpers. Before approving, both people compare a 12-digit safety code, so a forwarded invite cannot be used by someone else.

Removing someone replaces the profile’s key, so nothing added afterwards can be read with their old key. Everyone signs what they add, and each record shows who last changed it.

Record the appointment

With everyone’s permission (the app asks first; many states require everyone’s consent), record the visit and tap to mark a medicine change, an instruction, a follow-up or a question. The audio is encrypted into the profile when you stop.

Live transcription is used only when the browser can do it on the device; ordinary browser speech recognition sends audio to the browser maker, so it is never used. Otherwise, import a transcript from your phone’s recorder. Sentences about medicines, follow-ups, tests and warning signs are highlighted by simple word matching, each linked to its moment in the recording, with one-tap actions to add a medicine or set the follow-up date.

Bring the records you already have

Import the “Download my record” file most US patient portals offer (C-CDA XML, or a MyChart-style ZIP), SMART Health Cards (the signed vaccine QR cards from pharmacies and health systems, with an optional check of the issuer’s signature), FHIR R4 files, PDFs and photos. Duplicates are skipped, and the app warns when a file is about someone other than the profile you are importing into.

I checked the importers against the official SMART Health Cards specification examples, including verifying their signatures with the issuer’s published keys and rejecting a card with one character changed, and against HL7’s C-CDA example documents.

How the encryption works

Signing up creates an Ed25519 signing key and an X25519 key on the device. They are sealed into a keyring with AES-256-GCM under a key derived from both your passphrase (Argon2id with 64 MiB of memory, compiled from Rust to WebAssembly) and a random 128-bit Secret Key printed on your Emergency Kit. A server holding the sealed keyring therefore cannot try passphrases offline.

  • Each person or pet is a profile with its own key. Every change is a whole-record operation, encrypted, padded to a size bucket so record types do not show, and signed by its author.
  • The newest operation per record wins on a hybrid logical clock, so phones, laptops and servers can exchange operations in any order and converge.
  • A signed membership document lists who may read or write a profile and carries each member’s copy of the key, wrapped to their X25519 key. Servers enforce it; clients verify it and pin the inviter’s keys from the invite link.
  • Files are split into 256 KiB chunks, each encrypted with its own key and stored by hash. Shares are compressed, padded and encrypted with a fresh key that travels only in the link’s #fragment, which browsers never send to a server.
  • The interface uses DOM APIs only, with Trusted Types and a strict Content Security Policy, so an imported record cannot inject script.

Distributed, and easy to run

There is no Evolve Health company server. A storage node is one dependency-free Node.js program that stores ciphertext, relays invite replies and hosts shares until they expire. Run it on a home server, a Raspberry Pi or a small cloud machine, or use several for redundancy: docker compose up -d starts the app and a node together, and one more variable adds automatic HTTPS. Families sharing one computer can pass access between their vaults with no server at all.

Share health records deliberately

Life, disability and long-term-care insurers can price people on their health, and US genetic-privacy law (GINA) does not cover them. Evolve Health encrypts records on your device and does not add ads or analytics. Optional sync nodes store encrypted copies and can see connection metadata. Device or key compromise, exports and sharing can expose information; encryption does not guarantee immunity from breaches or legal demands. Sharing with an insurer, employer or lawyer preselects nothing and explains the stakes first; each share is labeled with its recipient and purpose. A treatment-only or no-redisclosure notice cannot itself prevent copying or bind every recipient.

It cannot stop an insurer from asking your doctors for their records when you apply, and it does not help anyone leave things off an application: misstatements can void a policy.

Checked

The tests run the real code paths rather than mocks:

  • Argon2id against the RFC 9106 test vector; tampered operations and forged membership rejected; two devices converging through a real node; sign-in on a new device with the Emergency Kit.
  • Caregiver invite, safety code, approval, contribution, role change and removal with key rotation, including a manager inviting a helper and failing to promote anyone.
  • Node policy: registration codes, quotas, erasure, expired or replayed request signatures.
  • FHIR R4 output checked for required elements and internal references, with appointment times preserved across three time zones and a lossless export-and-import round trip. The official HL7 validator has not been run yet.
  • The QR encoder matches an established library module for module, and a Chrome end-to-end test covers two people, a recording from a fake microphone, a hosted share being opened and withdrawn, and imports.

Limits

This is a working prototype, not yet independently audited. It is intended to organize records, not diagnose or treat; transcript highlights come from word matching, not medical judgment. It does not yet connect directly to providers’ systems (SMART on FHIR patient access would be next), bundle its own speech model, or offer time-delayed emergency access. Self-contained links and files enforce expiry only in the viewer, and people removed from a profile keep what they already had. Like every web app, you trust the site that serves the code; running your own copy removes that.
